# Chronicle Source Inventory

## Editorial scope

The initial lore timeline uses *World of Warcraft: Chronicle* Volumes 1–4 as its source backbone. These volumes organize research; they are not automatically modeled as four in-world eras. Era boundaries, dates, events, participants, outcomes, and causal links enter application data only after a human reviewer verifies the relevant citation locations.

## Timeline procedure

1. Record the physical or digital edition used and exact page/chapter locations.
2. Draft each assertion as a separate research Claim with its citation and confidence.
3. Derive in-world era boundaries from the reviewed chronology; do not use volume numbers as substitute dates.
4. Preserve conflicts or revisions as separate disputed or superseded claims.
5. Promote records to `reviewed` or `published` only after the citation checklist is complete.

No source scans, long excerpts, or copied book prose belong in public application data.

## Provisional coverage notes

These notes route research; they do not authorize a claim for publication.

- Blizzard's [Volume 1 announcement](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/19816948) describes its scope as the creation of the Warcraft universe through *Warcraft: Orcs & Humans*.
- Dark Horse's [Volume 4 catalog entry](https://digital.darkhorse.com/books/9b17a6faf3a945f5a2b96bcbe1846fad/world-of-warcraft-chronicle-volume-4) describes coverage from *Mists of Pandaria* through *Shadowlands*.
- Exact coverage for Volumes 2 and 3 still requires a physical/digital table-of-contents and page-index pass.
- Dragonflight and the Worldsoul Saga occur outside Volume 4's stated endpoint, so Era 9 needs first-party game and Blizzard sources in addition to Chronicle.

## Warcraft Wiki research index

Use the user-selected [Warcraft Wiki Chronicle Volume 1 page](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/World_of_Warcraft:_Chronicle_Volume_1) as the practical table of contents for topics, references, charts, and maps while the team does not have the complete book. Its linked [Black Empire article](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Black_Empire) is particularly useful for tracing the Chronicle-backed sequence and identifying later sources.

For Era 1, the index identifies the Chronicle map of Azeroth under Black Empire control; Warcraft Wiki's Old God references locate that map on Chronicle Volume 1, pp. 34–35. Treat those pages as the visual cartography reference during human review. The application may link to the wiki entry, but it must not bundle, trace, or hotlink the copyrighted book image without explicit redistribution permission from Blizzard.

The wiki is a secondary, community-maintained source. Apply these rules:

1. Keep records derived from it at `contentStatus: research` until a human confirms the cited source.
2. Record the wiki section and the underlying reference when available.
3. Prefer an accessible first-party source or the official Chronicle preview for the final claim citation.
4. Use hosted maps as geographic research references only. Create original, simplified atlas geometry and label any reconstructed extent as inferred or approximate.
5. Never copy wiki prose, Chronicle scans, or map images into the public application.
