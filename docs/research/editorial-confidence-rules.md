# Editorial Confidence and Publication Rules

## Evidence confidence

| Value | Use when | Public presentation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `explicit` | The cited source directly states the assertion. | State as sourced, without implying official project authority. |
| `strongly_supported` | Multiple details in the cited material support the assertion, but wording is not direct. | Label as strongly supported. |
| `inferred` | The assertion is an editorial conclusion from cited evidence. | Label as inference and include an editor note. |
| `speculative` | Evidence permits the possibility but does not establish it. | Label prominently; never merge it into an active factual claim. |

Confidence describes evidence strength, not importance. Reviewers must not raise confidence merely to simplify presentation.

## Claim status

- `active`: the assertion is the current reviewed account for this project.
- `disputed`: credible sourced accounts conflict; preserve each claim independently.
- `superseded`: a later reviewed source revises the account; retain the earlier claim and its provenance.

## Geographic certainty

- `exact`: the source supports the authored location at the atlas resolution used.
- `approximate`: the area is supported but not a precise point or boundary.
- `inferred`: placement is an editorial reconstruction and requires an editor note.
- `unknown`: no marker, route, or exact geometry may be authored.

## Promotion gate

Only a human reviewer may change `research` to `reviewed` or `published`. Promotion requires the citation checklist, original public prose, explicit uncertainty, and recorded reviewer/date. Active, non-speculative published claims must resolve to at least one Citation. Chronicle Volumes 1–4 are the initial timeline source scope; cross-volume conflicts remain visible rather than being silently reconciled.
